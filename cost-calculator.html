How to read the results

Transparent inputs. Separate outcomes.

ACash comparison

Annual recurring difference = (current monthly cost − proposed monthly cost) × 12.

Period difference = annual recurring difference × years − one-off transition costs. Positive means a lower proposed cash cost; negative means a higher proposed cash cost.

Simple payback = transition cost ÷ positive monthly difference. This assumes immediate savings and excludes time value of money, price changes and unentered costs.

BCapacity estimate

Annual hours released = weekly workflow hours × reduction percentage × working weeks.

Illustrative capacity value = hours released × loaded hourly cost. This is a way to value team time, not an additional cash return.

Test the whole workflow before accepting a time-saving assumption. Keep unknown costs visible rather than treating them as zero in a buying decision.

These results are scenarios built from your own assumptions. They are not promised savings, a Bunya quote or financial advice. Actual costs and any capacity released depend on your firm’s scope, data, licences and the way your team works.

Questions

Straight answers.

01Is this a Bunya quote?
No. It is scenario planning with your own numbers. Your proposal sets out the price for your firm, and nothing is built until you have agreed it.
02Is anything I enter saved or sent?
No. The calculator runs in your browser. It does not submit or save your entries, reloading clears them, and calculator entries are not sent with a demo booking.
03Why are cash and capacity kept separate?
Time released does not automatically reduce payroll, avoid a hire or generate revenue, so it is not added to the cash difference. Keeping them apart lets you judge each on its own.
04What time reduction should we assume?
Use your own assumption, and include checking and correction time in the current hours. Test the whole workflow in a pilot before relying on a time-saving figure.
